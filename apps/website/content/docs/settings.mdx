## Find your settings

Open the settings button in the header. The interface groups controls into
General, Network, Appearance, and Link profiles. On macOS, the interface also
provides the familiar Command-comma shortcut.

## General

Choose the default lifetime and optional download limit for new shares.
Existing shares should keep the values they were created with.
See [Expiration and limits](../expiration/) for the counting and expiration policy.

## Network

Port **8765** is the local file-download default. The separate developer
preview runs on loopback port **1420** and is not a file-sharing server.

Choose the network address your phone can reach. When a port cannot bind or an
interface disappears, FileHop reports the problem instead of claiming to be online.
A failed port change preserves the last usable listener.

## Appearance

System, Light, and Dark options let the interface follow your desktop or use a
specific appearance. The QR itself retains a clear light background for scanning.
**Hide QR codes and links** in General masks them until revealed, useful during
screen sharing. Hiding a code on your screen does not revoke its link.

## Window and tray

Closing the window hides it; sharing continues. Open FileHop again from the menu bar
(macOS) or the system tray (Windows, Linux), or click its Dock icon on macOS. The tray
menu shows how many shares are active and when the next one ends.

**Quit FileHop** in the tray, the app menu, or Cmd/Ctrl+Q stops all shares. While
shares are active, FileHop asks first, because their links stop working and running
downloads are cut off. After a restart, earlier links don't work.

The first time you close the window, FileHop explains this; turn the reminder off with
**Don't show this again** or under **General → Explain closing the window**.

On Linux desktops without AppIndicator support there is no tray. Closing the window
then quits FileHop, with the same confirmation while shares are active.

## Saved settings

Settings are saved on your computer and restored at the next start: port, network
address, default lifetime and download limit, theme, and the other options here.
Custom link profiles are saved too. Active shares, tokens, and shared file contents
are not persisted by FileHop. If the settings file is damaged, FileHop
keeps a copy next to it, starts with defaults, and tells you once.

## Keyboard access

Use Tab and Shift-Tab to move between controls, and Escape to dismiss dialogs.
Open **Commands** for searchable actions and **Settings → Shortcuts** for the list.
Use **Cmd/Ctrl+N** for an empty link, **A** to add files to the selected share, and
**T** to rename it. Native shortcuts, clipboard
images, and file saving need verification on each supported operating system.
